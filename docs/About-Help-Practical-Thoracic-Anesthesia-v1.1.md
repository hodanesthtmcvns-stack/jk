# Practical Thoracic Anesthesia: Core Skills to Crisis Survival

**About and Help** · Version 1.1 (17 September 2026)

---

## 1. About

### 1.1 Purpose

Practical Thoracic Anesthesia is an offline companion for residents in thoracic anaesthesia. It covers the full course of a thoracic case: preoperative assessment and risk, applied physiology, procedural skills, airway devices, common procedures, and the actions required in a crisis.

### 1.2 Author

Prof Jyotirmay Kirtania
Department of Anaesthesiology, Critical Care & Pain
MPMMCC & HBCH (Tata Memorial Centre), Varanasi

### 1.3 Intended users

Postgraduate residents and trainees in anaesthesiology, and anaesthetists who manage thoracic cases occasionally.

### 1.4 Important notice

This app supports clinical judgement and local protocols. It does not replace them. Check drug doses, thresholds and device-specific limits against current institutional guidance and manufacturer instructions before use.

## 2. Getting started

### 2.1 Opening the app

1. Save the file `Practical-Thoracic-Anesthesia-v1.1.html` to the device.
2. Open it in a modern browser: Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.
3. No installation, account or server is required.

### 2.2 Offline use

The app works without an internet connection. Only the web fonts need a connection; without one, the app uses system fonts and every function continues to work.

### 2.3 Adding to the home screen

- **Android (Chrome):** open the file, then Menu → Add to Home screen.
- **iPhone or iPad:** save the file to Files and open it. A home-screen icon requires the file to be hosted on a web address; Safari's Share → Add to Home Screen works only for web addresses.

### 2.4 Light and dark theme

Tap the moon icon at the top right to switch theme. The choice is remembered on that device.

## 4. Sections

### 4.1 Crisis (21 cards)

Crisis cards cover life-threatening, debilitating and unanticipated events during and after surgery.

- **Intraoperative (B1–B12):** hypoxaemia during OLV, sudden rise in airway pressure, poor lung collapse, difficult DLT intubation, anterior mediastinal mass collapse, massive haemoptysis, major haemorrhage, tracheobronchial injury, haemodynamic collapse, mediastinoscopy hazards, fire, blocker or tube caught in the resection line.
- **Postoperative (B13–B21):** cardiac herniation, post-pneumonectomy pulmonary oedema, bronchopleural fistula, intrathoracic haemorrhage, atrial fibrillation, lobar torsion, re-expansion pulmonary oedema, respiratory failure after extubation, local anaesthetic systemic toxicity.
- **Debilitating complications:** a summary table of chronic pain, airway trauma, nerve injury, positioning injury, venous thromboembolism, residual block and delirium.

**Card layout.** Each card has four parts:

1. **Recognise:** the cues.
2. **Memory items** (red box): the easiest, fastest actions that keep the patient alive. These come first.
3. **Then:** definitive steps once time is bought.
4. **Do not:** the error most often made.

Red cards are immediately life-threatening. Amber cards are serious but allow minutes.

**Using a card during an event:**

- Tap **Start** on the event clock when the crisis begins. The clock keeps running if you leave the card and return.
- Tap each memory item as it is completed. It turns green with a tick. Tap again to undo.
- Tap **Reset** in the memory-item box, and **Reset** on the clock, before the next event.
- Filter the list with the chips at the top: All, Intraoperative, Postoperative, Life-threatening.

### 4.2 Core skills (A1–A9)

Procedure pitfalls in the order of a case: preoperative planning, DLT selection, DLT insertion, fibreoptic confirmation, bronchial blockers, one-lung ventilation, monitoring and lines, analgesia and regional blocks, fluids and the pneumonectomy patient.

Each table gives the **pitfall**, **why it happens** and **prevention**. Read the middle column first.

- **Phones:** each table row is shown as a card.
- **Filters:** tables containing contested or outside-source points show filter buttons: All rows, Contested only, † outside sources.

### 4.3 Physiology (P1–P8)

Applied physiology and pathophysiology for thoracic anaesthesia: ventilation and perfusion from awake to open chest; shunt during OLV; hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction; atelectasis; ventilator-induced lung injury; the right ventricle and pulmonary circulation; bedside gas exchange; and pathophysiology that changes the anaesthetic. Each topic ends with a **key point**.

### 4.4 Preop (R1–R8)

Assessment pathway, cardiac risk (ThRCRI and timing after coronary intervention), lung function and exercise capacity, airway and mediastinal assessment, comorbidities, optimisation (smoking, anaemia, nutrition, fasting), prehabilitation, and a pre-anaesthetic checklist.

### 4.5 Thoracic Cases at a Glance (C1–C13)

One-page case sheets for common thoracic procedures, from rigid bronchoscopy to oesophagectomy and chest trauma.

Each sheet opens with a **header box**: Procedure, Time, Pain, Position, Blood loss, Practical techniques. Pain is graded **+** (mild) to **+++++** (severe). The header is followed by **Preoperative**, **Perioperative**, **Postoperative** and **Special considerations**, a **Related** line with links, and a **Sources** line.

### 4.6 Airway Devices for Thoracic Anesthesia (D1–D13)

Devices for lung isolation, airway access and oxygenation: left and right DLT, video DLT, single-balloon and bifurcated blockers, flexible and rigid bronchoscopes, videolaryngoscope, intentional mainstem intubation, laryngeal mask with blocker, airway exchange catheter, CPAP to the non-ventilated lung, and tubes with an integral or guided blocker.

Each card gives **Purpose**, **Practical use**, **Drawbacks**, **Evidence** (where available) and **Sources**.

### 4.7 Clinical lessons (8)

A principle and seven short lessons, each built on one common error. Each lesson has four parts: **Scenario → Mechanism → The catch → Take-home**.

- Swipe left or right, tap **Next** or **Back**, tap the progress dots, or use the arrow keys on a keyboard.
- **A− / A+** changes the reading text size. The size is remembered.
- **All** shows the whole lesson on one page; **Parts** returns to one part at a time.
- The **Related** line on the final part links to the matching skills, crisis, physiology, preop, case or device items.
- Some lessons end with a **My notes** box for personal reflection. Notes save automatically on the device. Do not enter patient identifiers.
- A tick replaces the lesson badge once every part has been read.

### 4.8 Drill

Self-testing on crisis memory items.

1. Choose a pool: All, Intraoperative or Postoperative.
2. Read the crisis title and cues.
3. Say the memory items aloud, in order.
4. Tap **Reveal memory items** and compare.
5. Rate yourself: **Got all**, **Partial** or **Missed**.

Items rated Missed appear most often, then unrated and Partial items; items rated Got all appear least often. The **Mastery** list shows the last rating for each crisis. **Skip** moves to another item without rating. **Open full card** shows the complete crisis card. **Reset scores** clears all ratings.

### 4.9 Search

Search finds any word or phrase across every section, including interactive tools.

- Type at least two letters. Several words narrow the results; every word must match.
- Subscripts do not matter: `ppoFEV1` finds ppoFEV₁ and `PaO2` finds PaO₂.
- Tap a result to open it.

### 4.10 Guide

The Guide holds the overview, the evidence legend, the crisis rule, the notes tools and the full reference list.

- **Copy notes:** copies all lesson notes to the clipboard.
- **Clear all app data:** removes every note, tick, timer, calculator entry and drill score from the device, after confirmation.

## 5. Evidence marks

Every significant statement carries a mark. Tap any mark in the app to see its definition.

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Established** | Verifiable anatomy, physiology or settled practice. Stated plainly. |
| **Graded** | Rests on trials, meta-analyses or formal consensus. The magnitude or consensus score is given in the text. |
| **Open** | The evidence is conflicting or inconclusive. |
| **†** | Drawn from general anaesthetic practice, not from the source literature listed in the Guide. Verify against local protocol before relying on it. |

## 6. Interactive tools

### 6.1 Shunt model (P2)

Two sliders set the share of cardiac output reaching the non-dependent lung and the reduction in that flow by collapse and hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction. Anatomical shunt is fixed at 5% per lung. The default settings reproduce the textbook estimate of 27.5% total shunt.

This is a teaching model, not a prediction for an individual patient.

### 6.2 Bedside gas-exchange calculator (P7)

| Input | Notes |
|---|---|
| FiO₂ | 0.21–1.0 |
| Barometric pressure | mmHg; default 760 |
| PaO₂ | mmHg |
| PaCO₂ | mmHg |
| EtCO₂ | mmHg; optional |

**Outputs:** PAO₂ (alveolar gas equation, respiratory quotient 0.8), A–a gradient, PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio, and (PaCO₂ − EtCO₂)/PaCO₂.

The last value is an end-tidal surrogate for dead space. It is not the Enghoff V<sub>D</sub>/V<sub>T</sub>, which requires mixed-expired CO₂. Follow its trend rather than its absolute value.

### 6.3 Thoracic Revised Cardiac Risk Index (R2)

Tap each factor that applies:

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| History of coronary artery disease | 1.5 |
| History of cerebrovascular disease | 1.5 |
| Planned pneumonectomy | 1.5 |
| Serum creatinine >177 µmol/L (>2 mg/dL) | 1 |

The tool shows the score and the risk of a major cardiac event: 0 points 0.9%; 1–1.5 points 4.2%; 2–2.5 points 8%; more than 2.5 points 18%. A score of 2 or more triggers a recommendation for cardiology evaluation.

### 6.4 ppoFEV₁ / ppoDLCO and the ACCP pathway (R3)

**Step 1.** Enter preoperative FEV₁ and DLCO (% predicted, 5–150).

**Step 2.** Specify the resection. Tap the lobes to be removed, or enter the number of subsegments (whole number, 1–42) for a segmentectomy.

| Lobe | Subsegments |
|---|---|
| Right upper | 6 |
| Right middle | 4 |
| Right lower | 12 |
| Left upper | 10 |
| Left lower | 10 |

Selecting all three right lobes is reported as right pneumonectomy; both left lobes as left pneumonectomy. Only one method is kept: entering a subsegment number clears the lobe selection, and tapping a lobe clears the number.

**Step 3.** Enter exercise results and cardiac status when the pathway asks for them: high-risk cardiac evaluation, stair-climb height, shuttle-walk distance, VO₂max (mL/kg/min or % predicted).

**Formula:** ppo value = preoperative value × (1 − subsegments removed / 42).

**Pathway (ACCP 2013 thresholds):**

- Both ppo values above 60%: low risk.
- Either ppo value 30–60%: stair climb (>22 m) or shuttle walk (>400 m); failure leads to CPET.
- Either ppo value below 30%, or high-risk cardiac evaluation: CPET.
- **CPET classification:** VO₂max below 10 mL/kg/min or below 35% predicted is high risk; 10–20 or 35–75% is moderate risk; above 20 or above 75% is low risk.

**Safety behaviours:**

- No postoperative values or risk category are shown until a resection is specified.
- Out-of-range, negative or fractional entries are rejected with a message; they are never silently corrected.
- An entered CPET result always determines the final category. If screening values alone would have suggested low risk but CPET is abnormal, the tool warns that CPET takes precedence.

Older rules use 40% as the high-risk cut-off. Confirm which pathway your thoracic team follows.

### 6.5 Prehabilitation triage (R7)

Tap each flag that applies: 6-minute walk distance below 400 m; Duke Activity Status Index below 34; reduced ppo values or VO₂max below 10 mL/kg/min (below 35%); current smoker; anaemia; nutritional risk; planned oesophagectomy.

The tool lists the recommended actions for the selected flags. The 6MWT and DASI cut-offs come from one published programme; the ACCP criterion is from the 2013 guideline.

### 6.6 Pre-anaesthetic checklist (R8)

Seventeen items in four groups: Risk, Airway and isolation, Optimisation, and Plan. Each item links to its source section. A progress bar shows completion.

Tap **Reset for next patient** before each new patient. No patient details are recorded.

## 7. Data storage and privacy

- All entries (memory-item ticks, event clocks, calculator inputs, checklist ticks, drill scores, lesson notes, theme and text size) are stored **only in the browser on that device**.
- Nothing is transmitted, synchronised or backed up.
- Data from one browser or device does not appear on another.
- Clearing browser data, private or incognito browsing, or Guide → Clear all app data removes the stored entries.
- **Do not enter patient names, hospital numbers or other identifiers** in any field or note.
- Use Guide → Copy notes to keep a copy of lesson notes.

## 8. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Action |
|---|---|
| Text looks different from expected | The device is offline or blocks web fonts. The system font is used; content and functions are unchanged. |
| Ticks, notes or scores have disappeared | Browser data was cleared, a private window was used, or a different browser or device is in use. Stored data does not transfer. |
| The ppo tool shows no risk category | Specify the resection (lobes or subsegment number) and check that all entries are in range. |
| The ppo tool shows "Check the inputs" | Correct the listed entries. Negative, fractional or out-of-range values are not accepted. |
| A calculator still shows old values | Entries are remembered on purpose. Use the Reset button in the tool. |
| The file will not open on iPhone | Open it from the Files app in Safari, or send it to yourself by email and open the attachment. |
| Swipe does not move a lesson | Swipe on the lesson text, not inside a notes box, or use the Next and Back buttons. |
| Search finds nothing | Use a shorter or single word, such as "pneumo" or "cuff". |
